BHAVYA FOUNDATION
AI LEARNING PLATFORM + PHYSICAL AI LAB
PRODUCT REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT

Status: Draft v1
Purpose: Product definition for the first major digital initiative
Source hierarchy: Foundation governance sources + approved logo; reference images are visual references only and their data is dummy.

1. PROBLEM

Students in rural Himachal Pradesh may have interest and potential in artificial intelligence but lack access to computers and structured opportunities to learn and experiment with AI.

Bhavya Foundation intends to address this access gap through a combined digital and physical model:
- a public website that introduces the Foundation and its AI education mission;
- a free AI learning platform with structured learning from foundation through expert levels;
- physical AI Labs where students without computers can access computing resources and turn online learning into practical experimentation.

The product is therefore an education ecosystem, not merely an informational NGO website or a course catalogue.

Evidence currently available:
- the product vision identifies lack of computer access as the central barrier;
- Foundation governance material identifies ethical AI education and AI Labs;
- Foundation governance material identifies AI-enabled learning and knowledge access.

Quantitative user research, student interviews, baseline learning outcomes, and demand measurements have not been provided. These are open discovery items.

2. PRODUCT VISION

Give motivated students in rural Himachal Pradesh the opportunity to learn, experiment with, and build with AI for free, regardless of whether they own a computer.

The desired journey is:

Discover → Register → Learn → Practice → Experiment → Build → Use AI Lab → Receive mentorship → Progress

The long-term product should connect online learning, practical computing access, projects, mentorship, and deeper AI education.

3. GOALS

Primary goals:
- Make core AI education free to students.
- Provide a coherent learning pathway from Foundation to Expert.
- Make learning practical rather than purely passive.
- Connect online learning with physical AI Labs.
- Build a durable institutional knowledge and education platform.
- Teach responsible AI as part of AI education.
- Design for students who may have phones but no personal computers.
- Create a maintainable foundation that can grow with the Foundation.

4. NON-GOALS

Initial scope does not include:
- implementing every Foundation programme;
- replacing governance or legal documents with software;
- inventing legal requirements;
- treating reference/mockup data as real Foundation data;
- building every advanced AI capability immediately;
- building large-scale infrastructure before demand is validated;
- charging students for core learning;
- publishing unreviewed AI-generated material as official Foundation curriculum.

5. TARGET USERS

Primary:
Students in rural Himachal Pradesh who want to learn AI.

Working assumption:
Some students may have smartphone access but no personal computer; exact device/connectivity patterns must be validated.

Secondary:
- students using physical AI Labs;
- mentors/instructors;
- Foundation administrators/curriculum managers.

Exact age range, language needs, accessibility requirements, and operational roles remain open questions.

11. AI LEARNING ASSISTANT

A future learning assistant may:
- explain concepts;
- provide hints;
- help debug code;
- generate practice questions;
- adapt explanations;
- support experimentation.

It must not replace authoritative curriculum, mentors, or human oversight.

AI-generated responses must remain distinguishable from approved Foundation educational content.

12. RURAL ACCESS REQUIREMENTS

The experience should be designed around constrained access rather than assuming high-end devices and connectivity.

Priority considerations:
- mobile-first public experience;
- efficient pages;
- low-bandwidth awareness;
- resumable learning;
- persistent progress;
- accessible typography;
- minimal unnecessary media/animation;
- offline/download capabilities where appropriate.

These are product requirements to validate against real field conditions.

13. SAFEGUARDING

The platform may serve children and youth.

Product design must align with the Foundation's Child Protection Policy.

Required design considerations include:
- age-appropriate experiences;
- consent/guardian processes where applicable;
- safe communication;
- mentor access controls;
- reporting mechanisms;
- privacy;
- controlled publication of student work;
- appropriate moderation;
- safeguarding incident procedures.

Exact implementation must be approved against the Foundation's governing requirements and appropriate professional/legal guidance.

18. ARCHITECTURE PRINCIPLES

Use a maintainable modular architecture.

Separate:
- public website;
- learning domain;
- curriculum/content;
- identity;
- progress;
- projects;
- labs;
- AI services;
- knowledge/library;
- governance;
- safeguarding;
- audit;
- analytics.

AI providers should be isolated behind replaceable interfaces.

Do not create a microservice architecture unless actual requirements justify it.

Use the filesystem/context architecture described by ICM for agent-assisted workflows where useful:
- numbered stages;
- stage-specific CONTEXT.md;
- reference/output separation;
- human review gates;
- inspectable artifacts.

19. RISKS

Digital access:
Students may lack computers and/or reliable connectivity.
Mitigation: physical labs plus low-bandwidth design and field validation.

Content quality:
AI education evolves quickly.
Mitigation: versioned content, review, approval, retirement.

Safeguarding:
Students may be minors.
Mitigation: safeguarding-by-design and policy alignment.

AI reliability:
AI assistants may produce incorrect information.
Mitigation: authoritative reviewed curriculum plus controlled AI assistance.

Lab sustainability:
Physical labs require equipment, maintenance, supervision, connectivity, power, security, and recurring funding.
Mitigation: define a sustainable operating model before scaling.

Premature complexity:
The broad mission could lead to overbuilding.
Mitigation: validate MVP with real students and expand incrementally.

21. NORTH-STAR OUTCOME

A student in a rural village in Himachal Pradesh, who may not own a computer, can discover Bhavya Foundation, create a free account on a phone, learn AI from foundational concepts toward advanced capability, visit a Bhavya AI Lab to experiment on real computers, build meaningful projects, receive mentorship, and continue progressing.

The product succeeds when the distance between “I want to learn AI” and “I can actually build with AI” becomes dramatically smaller for students who previously lacked access.
